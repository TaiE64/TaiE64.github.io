  • CasCVS-Net: A Staged Multi-Task Cascade for Critical View of Safety Assessment
    Bock-Zien Toh†, Yuanchuan Ren†, Tay Aw Yu, Ng Khee Ong, Zhehua Mao, Sophia Bano
    MICCAI 2026 SafeSurg Workshop · Oral
    arXiv PDF
    67.2CVS mAP
    +4.0mAP over LG-CVS
    15.3rare-anatomy mIoU
    Problem
    Automated Critical View of Safety (CVS) assessment in laparoscopic cholecystectomy depends on locating small, rare and often occluded hepatocystic structures.
    Approach
    A staged multi-task cascade for detection, segmentation and CVS classification: predicted boxes guide segmentation, and predicted masks supply region features for CVS, so inference uses no ground-truth annotations. Training grows from detection to the full three-task cascade.
    Results
    On the unseen Endoscapes test set: 67.2 CVS mAP, 32.0 detection mAP, 46.8 segmentation mIoU. It beats matched single-task baselines on all three tasks, and beats LG-CVS and SV2LSTG by 4.0 and 2.9 CVS mAP.

  • Calf-Integrated Arms for Bimanual Quadruped Loco-Manipulation
    Yan Pan, Yuanchuan Ren, Chipui Chan, Jingcheng Sun, Chengxu Zhou
    CLAWAR 2026 · Oral
    arXiv PDF
    2calf-mounted arms
    4feet stay planted
    3bimanual tasks in sim
    Problem
    Quadruped loco-manipulation usually trades manipulation against stance: trunk arms sit high and are single, leg manipulators lift a foot, and bimanual tasks force the robot to rear up.
    Approach
    Each front calf of a Unitree Go2 gets an arm (prismatic slider, two revolute joints, gripper), so the robot grasps at ground level with both hands while standing on all four feet. A vision-language model picks skills from a fixed library for long-horizon tasks.
    Results
    In simulation: a long-horizon cabinet task with autonomous skill selection, a cooperative two-handed lift, and an inter-arm handover.

  • RGBSQGrasp: Inferring Local Superquadric Primitives from Single RGB Image for Graspability-Aware Bin Picking
    Yifeng Xu, Fan Zhu, Ye Li, Sebastian Ren, Xiaonan Huang, Yuhao Chen
    IROS 2025 RGMC Workshop · Best Workshop Paper Award
    arXiv PDF
    92%real-world grasp success
    RGB onlyno depth sensor
    Best PaperIROS 2025 RGMC Workshop
    Problem
    Bin picking suffers from occlusion; CAD-based methods do not generalise to unseen objects, and RGB-D grasp regression is hurt by depth noise.
    Approach
    Recover superquadric shape primitives from a single RGB image using a foundation metric-depth model, then sample grasps guided by the fitted superquadrics. The pipeline includes dataset generation, point-cloud estimation and a global-local superquadric fitting network.
    Results
    92% grasp success in real-world packed bin-picking experiments, with good generalisation to unseen objects.
